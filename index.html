LESSON 01 · THE FIRST STEP

Meet your guitar.

Before playing songs, make friends with the six strings. In standard tuning, they are E – A – D – G – B – e, from the thickest to the thinnest.

STANDARD TUNING

Remember: E A D G B E

Tap a string to hear its note. Lowercase e = the higher-pitched E.

LESSON 1
♡ Easy memory trick: Every Amateur Does Get Better Eventually. The thickest E is the lowest sound; the thinnest e is the highest.
PLAY WITH IT

Your interactive fretboard

A fret raises a string by one semitone. Try the 5th fret on the low E string—it's A!

Tap any fret to see and hear its note.

Tip: The dots you usually see on a real guitar help you find frets 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12. At fret 12, the note repeats one octave higher.
A LITTLE MUSIC THEORY

Major, minor & sharp — what's the difference?

A

Major chords

Often sound bright or settled. Examples: A, C, D, E and G. A major chord uses a root, major third and perfect fifth.

Am

Minor chords

Often sound more reflective. The little “m” means minor—like Am, Dm, Em and Bm. Minor chords use a minor third.

F♯

Sharps (♯)

A sharp raises a note by one semitone. F♯ is one fret above F. A chord like F♯m is F-sharp minor—not a separate difficulty level.

LESSON 02 · YOUR CHORD JOURNEY

Small steps. Big sound.

Start with easy open chords. B and Bm introduce barre shapes, and F is a common next challenge. Everyone's hands are different, so the levels are only a guide.

How to read the diagrams: the six vertical lines are strings from low E (left) to high e (right). ● = finger position, ○ = play open, × = don't play. The numbers on the left show which fret the diagram starts at.
